# Scaling Law for WARC-Source Curation (proposed form, not yet fitted)

We propose a Muennighoff-style data-constrained scaling law that adds a single per-source quality multiplier to capture the DCLM-vs-LLM_curated crossover. For source $s$ trained on $D$ tokens drawn from a pool with $D_\text{obs}^s(N_\text{warc})$ unique tokens (a function of WARC count, observed directly from the curation pipeline and *not* fit), we fit $L(N, D \mid s, N_\text{warc}) = E + A/{N'}^{\alpha} + B/{D'}^{\beta}$, where $N' = U_N + U_N R_N^* \,(1 - e^{-(N/U_N - 1)/R_N^*})$ models diminishing returns from over-allocating parameters past the Chinchilla-implied $U_N$, and $D' = q_s D_\text{obs}^s \,[1 + R_D^*(1 - e^{-(D/D_\text{obs}^s - 1)/R_D^*})]$ in the repetition regime ($D > D_\text{obs}^s$) or $D' = q_s D$ otherwise. The free parameters are universal $\{E, A, B, \alpha, \beta, R_D^*, R_N^*\}$ plus one quality multiplier $q_s$ per source (gauge-fixed at $q_\text{DCLM} \equiv 1$), so $\sim$10 total against several hundred $(N, D, U_D, s, N_\text{warc}, L)$ points already produced by ExpA/ExpB/ExpFM/expWARC `summary.json` files. The form makes one sharp testable prediction we care about: at fixed model size, the **compute ratio** at which it pays to switch from a high-$q$ / low-$D_\text{obs}$ source (DCLM) to a low-$q$ / high-$D_\text{obs}$ source (LLM_curated) is *invariant in WARC count* — the absolute crossover compute $C^*(N_\text{warc})$ scales linearly with $N_\text{warc}$, but the multiplier $C^*(N_\text{warc}) / C_\text{DCLM-knee}(N_\text{warc})$ collapses to the constant $(q_\text{DCLM}/q_\text{LC})\,(1 + R_D^*)$ regardless of pool size; any deviation from a flat multiplier diagnoses a specific physical mechanism (multiplier growing at small $N_\text{warc}$ ⇒ coverage-dependent quality where narrow WARC pools depress $q_s$; multiplier monotone in $N_\text{warc}$ ⇒ the per-WARC density ratio $\rho_\text{LC}/\rho_\text{DCLM}$ is itself $N_\text{warc}$-dependent because one curation pipeline's filter saturates earlier than the other).
