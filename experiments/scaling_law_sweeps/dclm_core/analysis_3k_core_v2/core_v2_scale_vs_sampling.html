The Core_v2 jump is mostly scale — but head-biased sampling is a real second effect

Re-running Core_v2 across the biased and random 3000-WARC corpora, at matched model sizes, cleanly separates the two forces behind the 10k-scale scores. Data quantity dominates; how you sample the pool matters too, at roughly a third the magnitude.

Because the random-3k and 10k corpora share the same uniform-random draw and differ only in size, random-3k → 10k isolates scale. Because biased-3k and random-3k share N = 3000 and differ only in draw, random-3k → biased-3k isolates sampling.

What this says about the original question

You asked whether the Core_v2 behavior at 10k was a scale effect or something you could have predicted all along from the sampling. It's mostly the former. Holding the sampling method fixed, going from 3,000 to 10,364 WARCs buys +10 to +19 Core_v2 points at 2.9B — the dominant term. The head-biased draw is genuinely better than a uniform draw at the same size (+2.5 to +8.6), so sampling was always helping, but it's a second-order correction, not the main story. Notably the ranking among baselines is stable across all three regimes: DCLM ≥ Nemotron ≥ Resiliparse throughout.

Reading caveats

  • Best-of-budget selection. Each cell is the max Core_v2 over the 3–8 evaluated budgets — an optimistic point estimate, and budget coverage differs between biased-3k and random-3k.
  • random-3k coverage is thinner. Only DCLM / Nemotron / Resiliparse were trained on the random draw, and Resiliparse-random is missing 998M — so the sampling column is blank there.
  • Scale ≈ sampling equivalence. random-3k → 10k mixes "more data" with "more unique tokens / more epochs"; both move together here and aren't separated.
  • 10k ≈ random by construction. The 10k pool's quality distribution matches the uniform-random 3k draw (verified earlier), which is what makes random-3k → 10k a clean scale axis.
