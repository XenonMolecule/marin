\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Pretraining Web Data Curation}
Our Spec-Driven extractors join a collection of prior work for proposing data curation pipelines from open web crawls.  Perhaps the most well-known among these are Google's C4 \citep{10.5555/3455716.3455856}, EleutherAI's Pile-CC \citep{gao2020pile800gbdatasetdiverse}, Falcon's RefinedWeb \citep{penedo2023the}, HuggingFace's FineWeb \citep{penedo2024the} and FineWeb-Edu \citep{lozhkov2024fineweb-edu}, AI2's Dolma-CC \citep{dolma}, DataComp-LM (DCLM) \citep{li2024datacomplm}, NVIDIA's Nemotron-CC \citep{su-etal-2025-nemotron} among several others \citep{Gokaslan2019OpenWeb, OrtizSuarezSagotRomary2019, wenzek-etal-2020-ccnet}.  Each pipeline slightly modifies and builds upon the filters, rules, and classifiers designed by the prior work.  Recently, synthetic data has been identified as important to the web data curation pipeline, where LLMs rephrase documents into more informative formats \citep{maini-etal-2024-rephrasing, su-etal-2025-nemotron, niklaus2026_the_synthetic_data_playbook_generating_trillions_of_the_finest_tokens}.

The most similar papers to our work identify text extraction as a bottleneck in the pretraining data curation pipeline and propose LLM-empowered solutions.  \citet{ma2025aiccparsehtmlfiner} propose MinerU-HTML, a pipeline that uses a 0.6B LM to classify if chunks of HTML should be kept or discarded during extraction.  They produce AICC, a 7.3T-token corpus using the same filters as RefinedWeb but with their extractor, and find that it improves performance on downstream benchmarks.  Another closely related work to ours is SCRIBES \citep{liu2026scribes}.  Instead of using an LLM to directly extract the text \citep{wang2025readerlmv2smalllanguagemodel} or classify content to keep \citep{ma2025aiccparsehtmlfiner}, SCRIBES uses an LLM to write scripts to extract the contents of a particular page akin to a custom resiliparse \citep{bevendorff:2018:resiliparse} for a particular subset of webpages with semi-structured content.  In contrast, our method replaces not just the extraction step but the complete data curation pipeline with an LLM and a declarative spec.

\subsection{Declarative Data Pipelines}
Spec-Driven Extraction replaces an imperative, multi-stage curation pipeline with a single \emph{declarative} artifact: the spec states \emph{what} belongs in the corpus and how it should be formatted, leaving the underlying mechanisms to the model. This mirrors a broader shift toward declarative specifications of model and data construction such as DSPy \citep{khattab2024dspy} and labeling functions for weak supervision \citep{10.5555/3157382.3157497, Ratner_2017, smith2022languagemodelsloopincorporating}, both of which enable domain experts to embed expert knowledge in a system without caring about the underlying mechanics.  Looking specifically at spec-driven AI development, \citet{viswanathan-etal-2023-prompt2model} devise a method for synthesizing and retrieving training data based on a seed prompt.  And model constitutions can be thought of as a spec for the alignment process \citep{bai2022constitutionalaiharmlessnessai}.

Our spec-driven pretraining data curation also fits nicely into the broader literature on data documentation.  Artifacts like datasheets \citep{gebru2021datasheetsdatasets} and data statements \citep{bender-friedman-2018-data} are important steps towards documenting datasets, but they are authored separately from the pipeline. In contrast, our specs are self-documenting since they are directly responsible for the data pipeline behavior.
